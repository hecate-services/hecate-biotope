# Results: world 13

**The change:** an organ is tissue, and tissue is charged at the rate tissue is
charged at. `sensor_rent` and `hidden_rent` are deleted; a sensor weighs its
reach plus itself, a hidden node weighs one, and the apparatus is carried by the
same `carrying/2` expression that prices a body.

Corrects register entries **B.2** and **B.3**. One constant replaces two and is
**swept**: `neural_cost`, how much dearer a gram of neural tissue is than a gram
of body. **330 is the control and is worlds 2 to 12 exactly** — at the divisor of
33 it reproduces the flat rent of 10 a tick those worlds charged.

5 seeds, 2000 ticks, 100% efficiency, nine prices.

## A process failure, stated first

**This world was run before its criteria were written down.** The rules were
frozen in code before the first run, but no pre-registration document existed,
and that is not the standard every other world here was held to.

The result must therefore be read as **exploratory rather than confirmatory**.
What partly protects it is the design: a swept parameter with every value
published cannot be cherry-picked, which is the same defence world 7 relied on.
But the criteria were not fixed in advance and I am not going to write them
afterwards and pretend.

## The result

**Perception was never useless. It was unaffordable.**

| `neural_cost` | sensors/creature | hidden/creature | total reach | seeds dead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **330** (worlds 2-12) | **0.00** | **0.01** | **12** | 3 of 5 |
| 220 | 0.84 | 0.05 | 16 | 3 of 5 |
| 165 | 0.39 | 0.02 | 18 | 1 of 5 |
| 110 | 0.01 | 0.02 | 10 | 2 of 5 |
| **66** | **1.63** | **0.65** | **402** | 1 of 5 |
| 33 | 2.01 | 0.42 | 699 | 1 of 5 |
| 11 | 2.61 | 1.13 | 1181 | 0 of 5 |
| 3 | 2.96 | **2.68** | 2000 | 0 of 5 |
| 1 | 3.27 | 0.46 | 2506 | 0 of 5 |

Total sensor reach carried by the population runs from **12 to 2,506**. Hidden
nodes per creature from 0.01 to 2.68. **There is a step change between 110 and
66**, and above it this world looks exactly like every world since world 2.

**Extinctions fall with the price too**, three seeds in five down to none. A
world whose creatures can afford to perceive is also a world that survives.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**It settles the twelve-world null on perception.** Sensors and hidden nodes were
not being selected away for being worthless. A cell yields about 22 a tick and
one sensor cost 10 of it; the apparatus was priced out before selection could
express an opinion. That was a defect in the register — a flat fee where
everything else paid a rate — and not a finding about brains.

**It does not settle whether computation is USEFUL here.** The creatures now
carry sensors and hidden layers. Whether those networks compute anything that
earns its keep, rather than being cheap enough to drift, is a different question
and this sweep does not answer it. Drift is exactly what a nearly-free organ
produces, and the register has warned about that since world 4: *free organs mean
every creature grows every organ.*

**And it does nothing at all for G.1.** Every price, every seed, one founding line
of forty. Perception is not what holds a lineage.

## The honest reading of the number

Nothing derives `neural_cost`. Biology says neural tissue really is dearer per
gram — a human brain is about 2% of the mass and 20% of the budget, roughly
tenfold — which would put it near 33 on this scale if the analogy held, and there
is no reason it should.

So the finding is the **shape**, not the value: computation appears below a
threshold and is absent above it, and worlds 2 to 12 sat well above it by
accident rather than by argument. Choosing a value now would be choosing the
outcome. The sweep stands as the record.

## What this world teaches

- **Twelve nulls can share one cause, and the cause can be a pricing error.**
  Every results file since world 2 recorded perception at the floor and reasoned
  about why nothing needed to see. The answer was arithmetic and it was in the
  register the whole time, as B.2 and B.3, both marked *no physics settles it*.
- **A flat fee among rates is the same defect as a law applied at one site.**
  This is the fourth instance of that pattern after C.6, B.7 and B.8, and the
  first where the inconsistency was priced rather than structural.
- **Run the criteria first.** The one world here whose result most deserved to be
  confirmatory is the one I ran without them.
